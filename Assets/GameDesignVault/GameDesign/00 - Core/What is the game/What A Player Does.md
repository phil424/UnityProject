# What A Player Does

This is the player journey, it could be useful for understanding where dialogue, story events, discoveries and character interactions could naturally happen?

# 1. Preparation

Before an expedition, the player chooses the character they want to use (eventually this screen should feel like a character showcase rather than the boring menu it is now)

The player should be able to understand:
- who this character is
- how they fight
- their abilities
- their progression
- what they are currently working towards

The player may also have a persistent Progression Target... for example:
> I want this Character Mastery upgrade.

and that upgrade might require:
- some common resources
- a rarer Boss resource
- a Hunt requirement
- another special condition

That should give the player a reason to enter the next expedition... hopefully :P

---

# 2. Enter The Expedition

The character enters the map/region, the world is already active... There may be:
- ambient enemies
- available Encounters
- changing World Time
- future events
- Objectives

The player does not necessarily enter thinking:
> I must survive for 30 minutes.

They might enter thinking:
> I need another 20 common resources and one early Boss resource.

That could be a perfectly legit three minute expedition.

---

# 3. Short Term Objectives

Objectives are intended to provide the immediate 1-3 minute motivation.
Examples:
- clear two Horde Encounters
- kill an Elite
- collect a resource
- find an object
- reach somewhere
- progress a Hunt
- defeat a certain enemy type
- survive something
- complete a particular challenge

Objectives should affect what the player actually chooses to do... If the player needs to clear Horde Encounters and sees the following encounters available:
> Horde
> Elite Patrol
> Arcane Site

The Horde means progression, while the others are interesting choices... The player now has a choice and knowledge rather than simply pressing another encounter button because it exists... They could decide to extend their immediate horde objective to do an arcane site etc

---

# 4. Encounter Selection

The player can choose where the character can go. Naturally via automatic movement they may trigger encounters by simply travelling the map... but the player has agency over selecting encounters, eventually they might communicate things like:

**Threat**
How dangerous is this?

**Rewards**
Is this mainly useful for:
- resources
- Character growth
- an Ability
- a Weapon Mod
- something rare?

**Objective / Hunt Relevance**
Does this help something I am currently pursuing?

**Urgency**
Will this opportunity disappear?

The exact reward does not always need to be revealed. The player just needs enough information to make a meaningful choice.

---

# 5. Combat

The character travels and fights mostly autonomously, this is where the player watches and chooses when to interfere.

The player might:
- Charge away from danger
- knock enemies out of position
- heal
- use a major ability
- eventually change targeting behaviour
- abandon one Encounter and redirect somewhere else

Combat could create little stories naturally, but the player should regularly see:
> That happened because I made that decision.

---

# 6. The Run Build

The player earns temporary progression and their build begins changing... Maybe they intended to do a quick resource expedition... Then they find something like:
> Undead resources are doubled for the next three Encounters.

Now their priorities can change based on updated information.

Or:
> The next Ability Encounter has improved rewards.

Now they start hunting an Ability Encounter.
>Or a rare Encounter appears.
>Or they discover something connected to a Hunt.
>Or their current build suddenly becomes extremely strong.

The original three minute plan can organically become a ten minute expedition can become a 2+ hour session... The game should tempt the player into continuing rather than forcing them to commit to something.

---

# 7. Deeper Expedition

As the player remains in the expedition:
- their temporary build gets stronger
- opportunities can become more valuable
- tougher enemies appear
- World Events may occur
- rarer resources can become available
- Elites and Bosses matter more
- the world gradually becomes more dangerous

Long runs should be worthwhile but they should not make short runs pointless... and vice versa... shorter runs should not invalidate longer ones for more optimal resource gain etc

The player who only needs an early resource should still be able to enter, accomplish that goal and leave.

The player with an incredible build should have good reasons to continue.

---

# 8. Returning from an expedition

The player can voluntarily return from an active expedition at any time (at the moment there is no concept of extraction)

Its not intended to be an extraction looter system... but that doesn't mean it cant become that

The player shouldn't have to gamble everything they have earned/collected when they decide whether to continue... What has been earned remains earned. (mainly because I struggle with the games like dark souls and hollow knight where you lose everything and have to get it back)

The real decision should be:
> I have what I came for. Do I want to go back and use it?

versus:
> This build and these opportunities are too good to abandon yet.

Returning ends the temporary expedition build.

---

# 9. Failure

Death also ends the expedition, the temporary build is gone, the current expedition world state is gone... But already earned persistent ownership is not simply deleted as punishment.

Failure should mean:
> This attempt ended.

not:
> The last hour of my life has been confiscated.

The lost value is mainly:
- the temporary build
- unfinished goals
- opportunities the player never reached
- how far the expedition might have gone

---

# 10. Back to prep for expedition

The player returns to the long term progression.

They may now be able to:
- buy or unlock something
- progress a Character
- unlock a Weapon Mod
- unlock an Ability
- progress a Hunt
- unlock another Character
- discover something new

That naturally creates the next thought:
> Okay... now I want to try this.

and another expedition begins.

That is the full loop:
```
Preparation
↓
Choose a Goal
↓
Enter Expedition
↓
Objectives Give Direction
↓
Choose Encounters
↓
Fight / Intervene
↓
Develop Temporary Build
↓
Discover New Opportunities
↓
Return When Satisfied OR Keep Pushing
↓
Persistent Progress
↓
New Goal
↓
Another Expedition
```
