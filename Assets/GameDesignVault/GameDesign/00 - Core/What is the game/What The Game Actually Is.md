# What The Game Actually Is

This is short-ish version of what the game is supposed to be.

## The Basic Idea

Its a run based autobattling game, the character handles most normal fighting and movement automatically.

The player is not constantly controlling every swing or movement input... Instead, the player is watching what is happening, making strategic decisions, choosing where to go, deciding what opportunities are worth pursuing and using abilities at important moments to influence the fight.

An example:
>Enemies surround the character... They begin committing to attacks and get stuck in an attack animation
>
>The player uses Charge to escape.
>
>The enemies finish their attacks and completely miss... That kind of interaction is a part of what makes the game engaging and not a purely idle game

The automatic gameplay creates a situation that the player can see and understand and with intervention can change the outcome

---

# The Main Fantasy

The basic fantasy is:

> Pick a character I understand and enjoy.
>
> Enter a dangerous changing world.
>
> Decide what I want from this expedition.
>
> Build the character into something increasingly ridiculous.
>
> Hunt the enemies, resources or opportunities I currently care about.
>
> Decide whether I have achieved enough to return or whether this run is going too well to stop.

The game should support both:

> "I'll just do one quick expedition, grab enough for 1 more upgrade..."

and:

> "This build is absolutely insane and I'm staying until something kills me."

Both are valid ways to play.

---

# Characters

The player controls one "main playable" character per expedition. (Support characters are still a thing that could happen? Maybe a hunter with pets or something cool?)

Each character should feel different before the run starts.

A character has their own:
- visual identity
- weapon / basic attack style
- stats
- combo behaviour
- core abilities
- native Weapon Mods
- progression
- personality

Each character comes with their own set of fixed abilities (1 Heal/sustain, 1 Signature/unique, 1 utility) but these do not have to be shared across characters... For example, one character's Sustain might be direct healing while another character could recover through dealing damage, shields, regeneration or some other mechanic.

---

# Combat

Combat is mainly autonomous the character handles normal attacks and target pursuit the player handles encounter selection, ability activation and buildcraft

---

# Buildcraft

Buildcraft is one of the main reasons to replay the game. The player should begin with a direction, informed by their character, ability and weapon mod choices.

During an expedition they may gain things such as:
- temporary stats
- Abilities
- Ability upgrades
- Evolutions
- Weapon Mod interactions
- temporary buffs
- synergies
- other run specific effects

Most of the dramatic power growth happens during the expedition.

That temporary builds disappears when the expedition ends, a great run should eventually be capable of becoming completely ridiculous.

---

# Permanent Progression

There is also progression between expeditions.

The important principle is:

> permanent progression should increasingly give the player more possibilities, not reduce the game to "make their numbers so high that the game has no challenge anymore"

Permanent progression may unlock things such as:
- New characters
- Character Mastery
- Weapon Mods
- Abilities
- New build possibilities
- Progression branches
- Hunts
- Discoveries

There will also be persistent resources used for progression. Exact currencies, names and numbers are still flexible...

---

# The World

An expedition takes place in a region containing:
- Normal ambient enemies (enemies not in any specific encounter)
- Encounters
- Elites
- Bosses
- Changing World Events (Everything on fire, or everything dealing lightning damage etc etc)
- A day / time cycle
- Escalating danger (Linked to the day cycle and challenge)

The player is regularly given encounters and can decide what they want to pursue.

Eventually those choices should tell the player useful things such as:
- how dangerous something is
- what kind of reward it offers
- what resources it is good for
- whether it progresses an Objective
- whether it matters to a Hunt
- whether the opportunity is temporary

# A kind of core design principle

The game feels like it needs to have goals and a reason to play... So I've kind of boiled that down into these kind of time windows and milestones for the game...

10–30 seconds:
> What can I do right now?

30–60 seconds:
> What should I fight next?

1–3 minutes:
> What am I trying to accomplish?

3–10 minutes:
> Where is this build / expedition going?

10–30+ minutes:
> How far can I push this?

Across multiple expeditions:
> What am I unlocking, discovering or working towards?

Not every expedition needs to reach every stage... a satisfying three minute expedition is completely valid.
