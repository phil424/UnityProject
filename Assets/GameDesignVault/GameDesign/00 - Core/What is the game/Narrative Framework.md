# Narrative Framework

The detailed story is not currently written and themes are not locked in mostly!

That has been deliberate to allow for flexibility in development.

This document is about the structure the narrative needs to fit around rather
than telling you what the narrative has to be.

# The Main Narrative Idea

There is intended to be a progressive discovery mechanic hidden inside the repeated
expedition structure.

At first, the player may reasonably think:
> This is just how runs work because this is a roguelike.

But the game might appear shallow (not a lot of maps, not a lot of characters or enemies etc) so I would like to explore the possibility of deepening the narrative delivery through mechanics like objectives to give purpose to continue to play the game.

The current favourite idea is:
The characters themselves can have a different understanding of the game world outside of player assumption.

The character may gradually begin noticing and comment on things such as:
- places repeating
- events feeling familiar
- parts of the world apparently resetting
- remembering things they should not remember
- inconsistencies between expeditions
- information discovered by other playable characters

Over time it should become increasingly clear that the repetition itself is
part of the world/story.

Exactly WHY this is happening is completely open. 

Limited only by my access to free and cheap 2D pixel art assets :D

# What Is Fairly Firm

These are gameplay realities the narrative should probably work with rather than
attempt to remove. Change can always happen as well etc, so don't feel limited to these... we can make more and change things!

## Expeditions Repeat

Characters repeatedly enter temporary expeditions.

The region/world state can reset between expeditions.

---
## Some Things Persist

Not everything resets.

Persistent progression can include:
- character development
- unlocked Characters
- discoveries
- Hunts
- achievements
- abilities / Weapon Mods
- narrative knowledge

This means there is room for characters or the wider story to remember things
across expeditions.

---
## The Player Uses Different Characters

One playable character enters an expedition at a time. (But the opportunity for multiple IN and expedition is not out of the question etc) 

The game was designed initially for a party of multiple... but has evolved into 1... so multiple can exist, but only 1 is "playable" in terms of ability usage and triggering

---
## Combat Is Mostly Autonomous

The narrative should not depend on the character standing still every thirty
seconds for conversations. We have simulation speed controls so we can slow the game down and pause it etc, but it might look weird if everything stops to have a conversation etc...

The game is designed around continuous automatic movement/combat.

Dialogue and prompts can happen naturally while:
- travelling
- fighting
- approaching something
- finishing an Encounter
- seeing a landmark
- encountering an enemy

Longer conversations or major scenes are still possible when they are worth
stopping the game for, but we should discuss a whole system for something like this!

---
## Short Expeditions Are Valid

A player might only enter the world for a few minutes to accomplish one small
goal.

Important story content should therefore not assume every expedition lasts half
an hour.

Narrative progression may span many separate expeditions.

---
## Returning Is Safe

Choosing to return is a normal gameplay action.

It is not supposed to feel like the character narrowly escaping with stolen loot before everything is lost.

The fiction for returning is still open, but the gameplay intention is:
> I have finished what I wanted to do here.

not:
> I must escape before I lose all my rewards.

---
# Existing Narrative Ideas - Interesting, But not Locked

These are ideas already noted that i kinda liked, nothing is in stone though :D

### Characters slowly realise something is wrong
Early dialogue might be confusion rather than immediate lore

Things like:
> "Haven't we been here before?"

or characters recognising places/events without understanding why.

---
### An enemy eventually says something impossible

At some point an enemy could demonstrate knowledge they should not have. 
Example:
>Player unlocks Fire Weapon Mod - Super Effective against skeletons
>Random Skeleton:
>"You think that upgrading is enough to defeat us?!"

---
### A recurring enemy NPC may also be trapped
One concept is an enemy who believes their way out of the loop requires killing
the player.

That creates an antagonist who may initially appear evil while actually acting
on their own understanding of the same situation.

---
### Characters can talk about one another's expeditions
Example:
> Punchy discovers something.
> On a later expedition Ranger says something like:
> "Punchy wasn't exaggerating. This place really does..."

This makes unlocking/playing different Characters useful for narrative as well
as gameplay.

---
# Narrative Progression and Currency
This could be relevant.

The game may eventually have generic resource tiers that can be converted into
one another and characters could have narrative hooks embedded within their upgrade tree...

Story progression shouldn't necessarily rely on currency to unlock progression... but im not against it entirely... like i mentioned, there is going to be some cases where maybe a weapon mod for Ranger unlocks minions or something and that triggers some narrative thing etc

Some progression should require actually encountering particular content.

This gives us room for:
- Hunts
- specific bosses
- hidden encounters
- landmarks
- achievements
- NPC meetings
- character specific events
- discoveries

---
# Hunts
A Hunt is something that may progress across one or many expeditions.

Mechanically it could be something like:
> Collect three Idols -> Reveal a Boss -> Defeat the Boss -> Unlock something

But it could also carry narrative.

For example:
>First Idol
>-> strange inscription.
>Second Idol
>-> character recognises something.
>Third Idol
>-> enemy reacts to the player carrying them.
>Boss
>-> actual conversation/reveal.

The player gets gameplay progression and narrative progression from the same
activity.

---
# Objectives

Objectives mainly exist to answer:
> What am I trying to accomplish during the next few minutes?

Most objectives do not need to be story quests but the narrative can occasionally use the same structure.

For example, an objective could unexpectedly change from:
> Clear 2 Encounters

to something more unusual after a discovery.

Or a character may suddenly decide:
> Go back to that chapel.

The objective system gives narrative a way to insert into the game without requiring a different quest system etc

---

# Final thoughts

The framework I've built for the game shouldn't bottleneck narrative in anyway...

If a strong story idea requires changing something that is currently listed, that is completely fine... even some current working gameplay assumptions can be challenged. I don't pretend to be an expert :D

The things that are the most limiting in scope are playable characters and environments/levels. Really limited on player character choices and animations, if we want things like combo attacks and stuff...

Ill list a few links here to show some options I've found! But feel free to send me any more you find too!
