# Narrative Discovery & Loop Mystery

**Status: Exploratory**

# Intent

MiniCrawler is intended to eventually contain a progressive, partially hidden
story that the player discovers organically across expeditions.

The detailed narrative, mystery, characters and explanation for the expedition
loop are intentionally unresolved.

Narrative development should later be performed with dedicated writing work.

# Working Structural Direction

Characters may initially have limited understanding of:

- where they are;
- why expeditions repeat;
- why parts of the world appear to reset;
- what, if anything, persists between loops.

Contextual dialogue may gradually acknowledge:

- repeated locations;
- remembered events;
- inconsistencies;
- previous expeditions;
- discoveries made by other playable Characters.

Later progression may reveal that some enemies or NPCs also retain awareness of
the loop.

A recurring enemy NPC may, for example, initially believe that defeating the
player is required to complete or escape their own loop.

This is an example of structural possibility rather than confirmed narrative.

# Contextual Dialogue

Future dialogue may react to:

- current Character;
- previously used Characters;
- Region;
- Encounter;
- enemy family;
- Boss;
- Hunt state;
- Objective state;
- Achievement state;
- narrative discovery state;
- previous expedition events;
- World Time;
- other contextual information.

Different Characters may interpret the same discoveries differently.

Cross-Character dialogue may reference discoveries/actions from earlier
expeditions.

# Narrative Progression

Narrative/discovery state is distinct from generic economic progression.

Generic resources may eventually be convertible between tiers.

The following should not all be reducible to generic currency:

- knowledge;
- story flags;
- relationships;
- Hunts;
- Boss discoveries;
- authored encounters;
- NPC encounters;
- Character-specific discoveries.

This preserves reasons to engage with particular content.

# Delivery Surfaces

Potential future narrative surfaces include:

- ambient Character dialogue;
- contextual combat dialogue;
- enemy dialogue;
- recurring enemy NPCs;
- Hunts;
- Objectives;
- Achievements;
- Boss encounters;
- landmarks/discoveries;
- Character unlocks;
- Character progression;
- rare events;
- expedition results.

These are possibilities rather than implementation requirements.

# Delivery Principle

Narrative should generally emerge through normal gameplay where practical.

The mystery should be able to intrude into an otherwise systemic expedition
without repeatedly requiring the player to leave the gameplay loop for long
exposition sequences.

Larger authored conversations/cutscenes may still exist where they earn their
place.

# Long-Term Experience

A player may initially believe they are simply playing a systemic roguelike.

Over time the game may reveal that:

> the repeated expeditions themselves are part of a larger mystery.

The explanation for that mystery is intentionally unresolved.
