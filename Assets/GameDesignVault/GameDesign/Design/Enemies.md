# Boss Design
Principles:
- boss should interact meaningfully with autonomous combat + ability intervention
- readable moments for player abilities
- worthwhile repeat hunting
- compatibility with multi-boss escalation
- not every boss mechanic can assume a private arena.

That last one is particularly important because bosses may eventually overlap.

# Reward Identity
For each important monster/boss answer:
> Why would I want to hunt this again?

Potential answers:
- crafting materials
- weapons
- armour
- ability unlocks
- augmentation pools
- cosmetics
- challenge rewards
- variants
- collection progress.

# Threat Class vs Opportunity Rarity

Enemy combat importance and reward rarity are separate dimensions.

Threat might describe:
- Normal
- Elite
- Boss
- other future combat classifications.

Opportunity rarity / value describes how unusual or desirable an enemy is to
hunt.

A rare enemy does not necessarily need to be an Elite.

Likewise, an Elite is not automatically a rare sighting.

This separation allows encounters to create strategic decisions based on both
danger and potential reward.

# Rare and Valuable Enemies

Exploratory example:

## Nobleman Zombie

A rare zombie variant carrying unusually valuable rewards.

Possible identity:
- visually distinctive;
- uncommon sighting;
- valuable reward source;
- may appear as part of a specific encounter;
- surfaced prominently on the HUD / map.

The Nobleman does not need to be dramatically stronger than a normal zombie.

Its value comes from:
> "I want to hunt that target before the opportunity disappears."

This can cause the player to:
- redirect toward its encounter;
- change target priorities;
- abandon a lower-value fight;
- take additional risk for reward.
